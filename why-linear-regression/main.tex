\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Linear regression is the first model everyone learns and the one almost everyone underestimates.
This tutorial is not about running \texttt{lm()} in R or tuning \texttt{LinearRegression()} in scikit-learn --- it answers \textit{why} the method works: why squared error, why the normal equations, why the correlation coefficient appears inside a regression coefficient, why a straight line can measure \textit{correlation} yet cannot assert \textit{causation}, and why, more than two hundred years later, the $d$-dimensional hyperplane formula
$\widehat{\bm{\beta}} = (\X^\top \X)^{-1} \X^\top \y$
is still the most honest starting point of data analysis.
The only prerequisites are high-school calculus and basic linear algebra; every claim is derived from scratch, and all historical references are verifiable.
\end{abstract}
