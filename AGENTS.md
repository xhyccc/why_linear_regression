## 4. Knowledge cheat sheet (the basis for answering knowledge questions)

### Chapter 2 · One-dimensional regression

- No-intercept model $y_i = b x_i + \varepsilon_i$: $\hat b = \frac{\sum x_i y_i}{\sum x_i^2}$
  (convexity ⇒ unique global minimizer). With intercept: $\hat b = \frac{\Cov_n(x,y)}{\Var_n(x)}$,
  $\hat a = \bar y - \hat b \bar x$; the line passes through the data centroid; $x_i\equiv 1 \Rightarrow \hat b=\bar y$
  — **least squares is a generalization of the mean**.
- After standardization $\hat b = r\,\sigma_y/\sigma_x$ ($r$ = Pearson). Three facts: strength $|r|$ = steepness in
  sd units; $r$ is symmetric, regression is directional; $R^2 = r^2$.
- Galton 1886: $y = 0.516\,x + 33.73$ inches, $r \approx 0.5$ — "regression to the mean" is the arithmetic of $|r|<1$, not a biological law.
- Variance view: $\Var(y) = b^2\Var(x) + \Var(\varepsilon)$ (assuming $\Cov(x,\varepsilon)=0$ — **already a
  directionality assumption**); correlation = transferred variance.
- Directionality: $y\sim x$ and $x\sim y$ are different lines; product of regression coefficients $= r^2$;
  product of geometric slopes $= (\sigma_y/\sigma_x)^2$ (= 1 if standardized); neither regression line is the
  major axis (that is PCA, minimizing perpendicular distance).
- Causation: $x\to y$, $y\to x$, and common-cause $z$ all produce **the same $r$**; Reichenbach's common-cause
  principle enumerates, Wright's path analysis computes (given external causal knowledge); data alone can never
  decide direction. Reading a coefficient as a causal effect requires an identification strategy
  (randomization / natural experiment / no unmeasured confounding).

### Chapter 3 · The closed-form world

- Normal equations $\X^\top\X\,\bhat = \X^\top\y$; $\bhat=(\X^\top\X)^{-1}\X^\top\y$ (unique when $n\gg d$, full rank).
- Geometry: $\X\bhat$ is the orthogonal projection of $\y$ onto the column space; residuals $\perp$ column space.
- Gauss–Markov (Gauss 1823): under uncorrelated, zero-mean, equal-variance errors, OLS is BLUE.
- Why squared: convex quadratic ⇒ closed form; Gauss–Markov; link to normal MLE; Hessian $\X^\top\X\succeq 0$.
  Absolute loss (median regression) has no closed form: robust to outliers but non-smooth and inference-hungry.
- As $n$ approaches $d$: variance breaks first, rank second. Fix = ridge:
  $\bhat^{\mathrm{ridge}} = (\X^\top\X+\lambda\I)^{-1}\X^\top\y$, bias $-\lambda(\X^\top\X+\lambda\I)^{-1}\bbeta$
  (shrunk toward the origin, discounted per-direction by $1/(\text{eigenvalue}+\lambda)$). SVD view: ridge
  shrinks singular values by $s^2/(s^2+\lambda)$.

### Chapter 4 · Gradient descent

- Iterate (squared loss): $\widehat\bbeta_{t+1} = \widehat\bbeta_t - \eta\,\X^\top(\X\widehat\bbeta_t - \y)$;
  the fixed-point condition $\nabla L=0$ **is the normal equation**.
- Exact solution (combining closed form with iteration): $\widehat\bbeta_t = \bhat + (\I-\eta\X^\top\X)^t(\widehat\bbeta_0-\bhat)$
  — one-shot inversion = mortgaging the inverse into a degree-$t$ polynomial (Neumann-series partial sum).
- Convergence: $0<\eta<2/\lambda_{\max}$; optimal $\eta^\star = 2/(\lambda_{\min}+\lambda_{\max})$, rate
  $(\kappa-1)/(\kappa+1)$, iterations $O(\kappa\log(1/\varepsilon))$. "Standardize your features" upgraded from folklore to theorem.
- Three $\eta$ regimes (Figs. 5/6): $\eta\le 1/\lambda_{\max}$ monotone descent; $1/\lambda_{\max}<\eta<2/\lambda_{\max}$
  oscillating descent (sign flips each step, amplitude decays by $|1-\eta\lambda_{\max}|$); $\eta>2/\lambda_{\max}$
  oscillating divergence.

### Chapter 5 · Underdetermined & implicit regularization

- When $d>n$ the solutions form a manifold $\widehat\bbeta' + \mathcal N(\X)$: every point is an interpolating
  solution and a GD fixed point; the manifold attracts transversally, single points are only Lyapunov stable;
  the landing point is set by the null-space component of the initialization.
- Minimum-norm solution $\widehat\bbeta' = \X^\top(\X\X^\top)^{-1}\y = \X^+\y$.
  **Five faces of one solution**: two closed forms, the pseudoinverse, the limit of GD from zero,
  and the ridge $\lambda\to0^+$ limit.
- Arbitrary init: $\lim_t\widehat\bbeta_t = \widehat\bbeta' + \P_{\mathcal N(\X)}\widehat\bbeta_0$
  (null space is neutral).
- $\X^\top\X$ and $\X\X^\top$ have the same nonzero eigenvalues (with multiplicities) ⇒ the "$d$ formulas" and
  the "$n$ formulas" are interchangeable (effective dimension $r$).

### Chapter 6 · From estimation to inference

- $\bbeta = \Sigma_{XX}^{-1}\Sigma_{Xy} = -\Theta_{Xy}/\Theta_{yy}$, $\Var(\varepsilon)=1/\Theta_{yy}$.
- **Zero pattern of the precision matrix ⟺ conditional independence**: $\Theta_{ij}=0 \iff i\perp j\mid$ rest;
  partial correlation $\rho_{ij\cdot\cdot} = -\Theta_{ij}/\sqrt{\Theta_{ii}\Theta_{jj}}$;
  $\beta_j=0 \iff y\perp x_j\mid\x_{-j}$.
- Four faces of sparsity: combinatorial (NP-hard → tractable when sparse), geometric (convex hull of the
  $\ell_0$ ball is the $\ell_1$ ball), statistical (effective df $\widehat{df}=\E\|\widehat\bbeta\|_0$),
  philosophical (sparsity is an assumption about the world).
- Methods: lasso $\arg\min \frac1n\|\y-\X\bbeta\|^2+\lambda\|\bbeta\|_1$; elastic net
  $\lambda(\alpha\|\bbeta\|_1+(1-\alpha)\|\bbeta\|_2^2)$ (correlated variable groups); graphical lasso
  ($\ell_1$-penalized likelihood estimates the whole graph) and CLIME ($\bm S\Theta-\I\approx0$ solved
  column-by-column via linear programming; better conditioned, parallelizable, slightly less statistically
  efficient); nodewise lasso (Meinshausen–Bühlmann for graph structure).
- Debiased lasso: $\widetilde\bbeta = \widehat\bbeta_{\mathrm{lasso}} + \frac1n\widehat\Theta\X^\top
  (\y-\X\widehat\bbeta_{\mathrm{lasso}})$; when the bias term is $O_p(s\log d/n) = o_p(n^{-1/2})$ it is dominated by
  noise and per-coefficient asymptotic normality is restored ⇒ testable $p$-values and confidence intervals.
  **Estimation says which edges might exist; inference says which are significant.**

### Chapter 7 · FDR control

- Multiple testing: FWER = $\Prob(V\ge1)$, FDR = $\E[V/\max(R,1)] \le$ FWER. Bonferroni at $\alpha/m$: strong
  FWER control but power collapses as $m$ grows.
- **BH procedure**: $\hat k = \max\{k : p_{(k)} \le qk/m\}$, reject $p_{(1..\hat k)}$; under independence (or
  PRDS) $\mathrm{FDR}\le q$; Bonferroni's rejection set $\subseteq$ BH's. Under general dependence use the BY
  correction $q/\sum_{j=1}^m j^{-1}$.
- BH = adaptive sparse estimation (Abramovich et al. 2006): in the sparse normal-means model the BH threshold
  automatically attains the minimax rate of the oracle threshold, $2s\log(m/s)$ vs $2s\log m$ — **controlling
  FDR is the testing-problem dual of the sparsity assumption**.
- **Knockoffs** (Barber–Candès 2015): construct shadow variables $\tilde\X$ inside the design; $W_j = |Z_j| - |\tilde Z_j|$;
  under the null $W_j \overset d= -W_j$ (sign-flip symmetry); knockoff+ threshold
  $\min\{t : \frac{1+\#\{W_j\le -t\}}{\#\{W_j\ge t\}} \le q\}$. Model-free, finite-sample-exact FDR control.
  Scope: fixed-$X$ construction needs $n\ge 2d$; model-X Gaussian construction needs the feature distribution
  known/estimable (the book describes the latter).
- Two routes compared: BH + debiased lasso relies on asymptotic normality (approximate guarantee);
  knockoffs rely on exchangeability (exact guarantee, needs $d\le n$ or group-expansion tricks).

### Chapter 8 · Kernels and deep networks

- $\x_i=\phi(\z_i)$; kernel $k(\z,\z')=\langle\phi(\z),\phi(\z')\rangle$; $\K=\Phi\Phi^\top$ (no $\phi$ coordinates
  ever appear). Mercer: PSD kernel ⟺ some (possibly infinite-dimensional) feature map.
- $d>n$ (kernel interpolation / kernel ridgeless): $\hat f(\tilde\z) = \k(\tilde\z)^\top\K^{-1}\y
  = \sum_i \alpha_i k(\tilde\z,\z_i)$, $\bm\alpha=\K^{-1}\y$. GD from zero converges to the minimum-norm
  interpolating function in the RKHS (Kimeldorf–Wahba representer).
- $n\gg d$: direct kernelization impossible ($\Phi^\top\Phi$ depends on feature coordinates); ridge +
  **push-through** $(\Phi^\top\Phi+\lambda\I_d)^{-1}\Phi^\top = \Phi^\top(\K+\lambda\I_n)^{-1}$ ⇒ kernel ridge
  regression $\hat f(\tilde\z)=\k(\tilde\z)^\top(\K+\lambda\I_n)^{-1}\y$; as $\lambda\to0^+$ both regimes coincide.
  GP regression is the same object in Bayesian dress.
- "Naturalness" of kernels: polynomial kernel (finite-dimensional, convenient); Gaussian kernel ($d=\infty$,
  kernelization is the only way out); random Fourier features (Rahimi–Recht 2007, approximating the kernel
  back to finite dimensions).
- **Bridge 1**: two-layer net $f(\z;\a)=\frac1{\sqrt D}\sum_j a_j\sigma(\omega_j^\top\z+b_j)$, first layer frozen,
  zero initialization, GD on the second layer = **ridgeless regression in random-feature space**;
  RF kernel $k_{\mathrm{RF}}\to\E[\sigma(\omega^\top\z+b)\sigma(\omega^\top\z'+b)]$.
- **Bridge 2 (NTK, Jacot et al. 2018)**: $\Theta_t(\z,\z')=\langle\nabla_\theta f(\z),\nabla_\theta f(\z')\rangle$;
  in the wide limit $D\to\infty$, $\Theta_t\to\Theta$ stays constant ⇒ linear ODE in function space ⇒
  $f_\infty(\tilde\z)=\bm\Theta(\tilde\z)^\top\bm\Theta^{-1}\y$ — symbol-for-symbol isomorphic to kernel
  interpolation. Any depth: Lee et al. 2019. **The non-convexity of parameter space is an artifact of the coordinate system.**
- Pragmatic notes: NTK = lazy regime (no feature learning; theory for genuinely deep learning is under
  construction); double descent (Belkin 2019): test error keeps decreasing past the interpolation threshold,
  and the minimum-norm solution is its cleanest linear incarnation; Wu et al. 2025: optimally early-stopped GD
  statistically dominates optimal ridge.

### Chapter 9 · Epilogue (the "final answer")

Linear regression is the only model that is simultaneously: **exactly solvable** (convex, closed-form,
hyperparameter-free), **interpretable** (each coefficient, $R^2$, leverage, df have geometric meanings),
**auditable** (every theorem ships with an assumption checklist + named diagnostic fixes), and **extensible**
(change the loss → robust regression; add penalties → ridge/lasso; add features → nonlinearity; iterate
reweighting → GLM; stack → neural networks). Methodological slogan: fit the straight line first, audit the
assumptions, disclose the weaknesses honestly — and only then reach for fancier models.

### 4. 知识速查（中文对照，与上半区一一对应）

- **第 2 章**：$\hat b=\Cov_n/\Var_n=r\,\sigma_y/\sigma_x$；$R^2=r^2$；回归线过数据中心，是均值的推广；
  方差视角 $\Var(y)=b^2\Var(x)+\Var(\varepsilon)$（已含方向性假设）；$y\sim x$ 与 $x\sim y$ 是两条不同的线，
  系数之积 $=r^2$，都不是主轴（主轴是 PCA）；三种因果结构（$x\to y$、$y\to x$、共同原因）产生同一个 $r$，
  数据本身不能判定方向，读成因果需要识别策略（随机化/自然实验/无未测混杂）。
- **第 3 章**：正规方程与闭式解；投影几何；Gauss–Markov（BLUE）；平方损失的四个理由；$n$ 靠近 $d$ 时
  方差先坏、秩后坏，ridge 修复（偏差 $-\lambda(\X^\top\X+\lambda\I)^{-1}\bbeta$，SVD 视角收缩因子
  $s^2/(s^2+\lambda)$）。
- **第 4 章**：GD 迭代与不动点=正规方程；精确解 $\widehat\bbeta_t=\bhat+(\I-\eta\X^\top\X)^t(\widehat\bbeta_0-\bhat)$
  （一步到位=Neumann 级数部分和）；收敛域 $0<\eta<2/\lambda_{\max}$，最优 $\eta^\star=2/(\lambda_{\min}+\lambda_{\max})$，
  速率 $(\kappa-1)/(\kappa+1)$；三档 $\eta$：单调下降 / 震荡下降 / 震荡发散。
- **第 5 章**：$d>n$ 时解流形 $\widehat\bbeta'+\mathcal N(\X)$，流形横向吸引、单点仅 Lyapunov 稳定；
  最小范数解 $\X^\top(\X\X^\top)^{-1}\y=\X^+\y$ 五面孔同一解（两个闭式、伪逆、GD 从零极限、ridge $\lambda\to0^+$ 极限）；
  任意初值 $\lim_t\widehat\bbeta_t=\widehat\bbeta'+\P_{\mathcal N(\X)}\widehat\bbeta_0$；$\X^\top\X$ 与 $\X\X^\top$
  非零特征值相同 ⇒ $d$ 公式与 $n$ 公式可互换。
- **第 6 章**：$\bbeta=-\Theta_{Xy}/\Theta_{yy}$；**精度矩阵零元 ⟺ 条件独立**；偏相关
  $-\Theta_{ij}/\sqrt{\Theta_{ii}\Theta_{jj}}$；稀疏性四张脸（组合/几何/统计/哲学）；lasso、elastic net、
  graphical lasso、CLIME、nodewise lasso；debiased lasso 把偏差压到 $o_p(n^{-1/2})$ 恢复逐系数渐近正态
  ⇒ $p$ 值与置信区间；估计说哪些边可能存在，推断说哪些边显著。
- **第 7 章**：FWER vs FDR；Bonferroni 强控制但功效崩塌；BH 程序 $\hat k=\max\{k:p_{(k)}\le qk/m\}$，
  独立/PRDS 下 FDR$\le q$，BY 修正处理一般相依；BH=自适应稀疏估计（minimax 速率 $2s\log(m/s)$）；
  knockoffs 靠交换对称 $W_j\overset d=-W_j$ 做模型无关、有限样本精确的 FDR 控制（固定-$X$ 需 $n\ge2d$；
  书中为 model-X 高斯构造）。
- **第 8 章**：$\x=\phi(\z)$，核 $k(\z,\z')=\langle\phi(\z),\phi(\z')\rangle$，Mercer 定理；$d>n$ 核插值
  $\hat f(\tilde\z)=\k(\tilde\z)^\top\K^{-1}\y$（=RKHS 最小范数插值，GD 从零收敛点）；$n\gg d$ 靠 ridge +
  push-through 得核岭回归，$\lambda\to0^+$ 两 regime 殊途同归（GP 回归是贝叶斯叙述）；多项式核/高斯核/
  随机 Fourier 特征；桥一：两层网络冻结第一层训练第二层=随机特征 ridgeless 回归；桥二 NTK：宽极限下
  核全程不动，函数空间线性 ODE，$f_\infty(\tilde\z)=\bm\Theta(\tilde\z)^\top\bm\Theta^{-1}\y$ 与核插值逐符号同构；
  务实注记：NTK=lazy regime；double descent（插值阈值后测试误差继续降）；Wu et al. 2025：最优提前停止的
  GD 统计支配最优 ridge。
- **第 9 章**：线性回归是唯一同时可精确求解、可解释、可审计、可推广的模型；口号：先拟合直线，审计假设，
  诚实披露弱点，然后才轮到更花哨的模型。

## 5. FAQ seeds (canonical answer routes)

- **"Why is the slope a correlation?"** → Ch. 2: $\hat b=\Cov_n/\Var_n = r\,\sigma_y/\sigma_x$; $r$ is the slope in
  sd units; $R^2=r^2$.
- **"Why can't regression read off causation?"** → Ch. 2: three structures, one $r$; Reichenbach enumerates,
  Wright computes, identification lives outside the data.
- **"How can $d>n$ with zero training error generalize?"** → Ch. 5 (min-norm selection) + Ch. 8 (double descent,
  ridgeless, implicit GD regularization dominating ridge).
- **"What do kernels have to do with linear regression?"** → Ch. 8: linear in $\x=\phi(\z)$ ⟺ nonlinear in $\z$;
  both closed forms assemble only from kernels; the wide limit of deep learning is kernelized linear regression.
- **"Lasso or ridge?"** → Ch. 3 & 6: bias budget vs sparsity assumption; correlated groups → elastic net;
  graph structure → nodewise/glasso/CLIME; $p$-values → debiased lasso (Ch. 6) or multiple testing (Ch. 7).
- **"How should I threshold a page of p-values?"** → Ch. 7: BH (FDR control, far more power than Bonferroni);
  model-free exact control → knockoffs.
- **"What does the book cover / not cover?"** → Preface (a): GLMs, robust regression, mixed effects, time series,
  Bayesian routes, online learning, federated settings are all out of scope; only the "cannot-honestly-skip" main line is covered.

### 5. 常见问题回答口径（中文对照）

斜率为什么是相关系数 → 第 2 章；为什么不能读出因果 → 第 2 章（三种结构同一个 $r$，识别策略在数据之外）；
$d>n$ 零训练误差为何泛化 → 第 5 章 + 第 8 章；核方法与线性回归的关系 → 第 8 章；lasso 还是 ridge →

贝叶斯路线等均未覆盖，只覆盖「不答就不诚实」的主线。

## 6. Hard conventions when editing the book

1. Changing any chapter ⇒ **port the same change to the other language edition** (mirrored structure), rebuild
   both, both must show 0 errors.
2. Chapter/section titles carry a running-head short title: `\section[short]{full}` (all-caps English titles
   overflow the header width easily).
3. Never put commands containing `\bm` (e.g. `\widehat{\bm{\beta}}`, i.e. `\bhat`) into the **optional argument**
   of `\section/\subsection` (hyperref bookmark conversion throws `Illegal parameter number`); only simple math
   like `$d>n$` is allowed there.
4. Wide tables are wrapped in `\resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{...}` (the book has several three-column comparison tables).
5. Figures are PDF only; the text must never `includegraphics` a PNG/JPG.
6. Do not touch existing PDFs in `figures/` or `make_figures.py` unless the user explicitly asks to regenerate figures.
7. Commit messages: concise English; push target `origin/main` (github.com/xhyccc/why_linear_regression).

### 6. 编辑本书时的硬性约定（中文对照）

1. 改任一章节 ⇒ **同步改另一语言版本**并重新编译两版，均须 0 错误。2. 章节标题带页眉短标题
`\section[短标题]{完整标题}`。3. 含 `\bm` 的命令（如 `\bhat`）**不得**放进 `\section/\subsection` 的可选参数
（hyperref 书签会报 `Illegal parameter number`）；可选参数只允许 `$d>n$` 这类简单数学。4. 宽表格用
`\resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{...}`。5. 插图一律 PDF，正文不得引用 PNG/JPG。6. 不改动 `figures/` 下现有 PDF
与 `make_figures.py`，除非用户明确要求重生成插图。7. 提交信息用英文简明描述；推送目标 `origin/main`。
